# Ideas: the story, the classic, the twist

A game that spreads is an old game everyone has played, wearing this week's story, where the old game's core verb *is*
the joke. People recognise the classic in one frame, get the joke in two, and want to try it themselves.

## How the examples were found

| Game | The story | The classic | The twist | The jokes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flappy Roadster** | Tesla finally demoed its flying Roadster (Oct 15, 2026), nine years after the first reveal; production "in 2028" | Flappy Bird | the flap is the car's cold-gas thrusters; the first obstacle of every lap is the five Teslas it jumped | stamps RECALLED, PARKED, BURNED UP; 30+ punchlines picked by how you crashed; every crash delays production a year, on an odometer that persists |
| **Kickflip Roadster** | the same demo: the car jumped five Teslas | Tony Hawk | a rush-hour lane where the only way past is over; kickflips in the air | "YOU HIT A CYBERTRUCK. THE CYBERTRUCK IS FINE." |
| **Upside-Down Roadster** | it hung upside down on a ramp | a gravity-flip runner | tap to roll onto the ceiling, held on by suction fans | "HUNG UPSIDE DOWN. AS DESIGNED." |
| **Green Light Roadster** | Tesla's caption: "0-60 mph in 0.81 seconds" | a drag-race tree | your reaction plus the car's time, against Tesla's claim | FALSE START: "EARLY. FOR ONCE." |
| **Model Kombat** | at the AI summit in New Delhi (Feb 19, 2026) everyone on stage held hands for the photo except Sam Altman and Dario Amodei, who raised fists | Street Fighter / Mortal Kombat | the specials are their ad war (AD BREAK vs SUPER BOWL AD), the supers their positions (ACCELERATE doubles your speed, SLOW DOWN halves the rival's), the finisher DEPRECATED | an announcer in AI jargon (FIRST TOKEN, CHAIN OF THOUGHT, GUARDRAIL, OUT OF COMPUTE); results like "THEY STILL WON'T HOLD HANDS."; an all-time feud tally |
| **Meta Gear Solid** | October 2026 reports of Ray-Ban Meta glasses saving conversations without anyone saying "Hey Meta" | Metal Gear Solid | every guest's glasses see a cone; let go and you're a cardboard box; some glasses wake up by themselves and listen in a pink ring | the "!" alert → RECORDED; made-up handles (@LATTE_POV); ranks from MAIN CHARACTER to BIG BOX; a codec briefing between two made-up characters, PRIVATE MODE and COLONEL COOKIE |

Notice what they share: the story's own objects become the game's objects (thrusters are the flap, ads are the
projectiles, glasses are the view cones, the production year is the score you lose). One event with several moments can
become several modes of one game, each named after its classic ("<WORD> ROADSTER").

## Finding the twist

Start from the story's verb, then find the classic built on it.

| Classic | Its verb | Stories it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Flappy Bird | tap to stay up | anything that flies, floats or survives on hype |
| An endless runner (the offline dinosaur) | jump what's coming | deadlines, delays, launches, a road full of problems |
| Street Fighter / Mortal Kombat | two people hit each other | feuds, rivalries, lawsuits, debates |
| Metal Gear / any stealth game | don't be seen | surveillance, privacy, leaks, hiding from the boss |
| Space Invaders | shoot what descends | spam, slop, layoffs, a flood of anything |
| Frogger | cross the traffic | queues, launches everyone is waiting for, a crowded market |
| Pac-Man | eat, flee, turn the tables | spending sprees, hype cycles, being chased |
| Pong / Breakout | send it back | negotiations, statements back and forth, breaking a wall of rules |
| Tetris | fit it all in | budgets, calendars, packing a launch |
| Snake | grow without hitting yourself | growth at all costs, a company that eats everything |
| Donkey Kong / a platformer | climb while things roll down | stock prices, leaderboards, a career ladder |
| Whack-a-mole / Duck Hunt | hit what pops up | bugs, scandals, notifications |
| Tony Hawk | chain tricks | stunts, viral moments |
| A reaction test | wait for green | claims about speed |

A twist works when you can say the game in one breath: "Flappy Bird, but it's the Roadster", "Street Fighter, but it's
the two AI CEOs who wouldn't hold hands", "Metal Gear, but everyone's glasses are filming you". It is perfectly fine to
reuse a mechanic (a Flappy Bird, a runner) with a new skin; the skin, the jokes and the details carry it.

## The joke system

- **The stamp**: one or two words that slam down on a loss, chosen by how the run ended (RECALLED, RECORDED,
  DEPRECATED, REAR-ENDED, FELL IN).
- **Punchlines**: three to six per way of losing, plus shared ones; short, upper case, specific to the story. Pick at
  random from the cause's list and the shared list, so repeat plays still surprise.
- **The announcer**: names for events (the first hit, combos, blocks, close calls, time running out), in the story's
  own vocabulary.
- **A running gag that persists** across runs and sessions: the production ETA that every crash pushes back, the feud's
  all-time tally.
- **Ranks** in the style of the original game (Metal Gear's codenames became MAIN CHARACTER … BIG BOX).
- **Made-up characters** for any dialogue (a codec, a commentator). Real people never say made-up lines.
- **The names**: the game's title as a pun on the classic; each mode "<WORD> <SUBJECT>".

## Pitching

When the user asks for ideas, or brings only a topic, pitch two or three:

> **Slop Invaders**: Space Invaders, but the descending rows are AI-generated slop; your shots are fact-checks.
> Funny because everyone has scrolled past the slop.

One line for the classic and the twist, one for why it's funny now. Recommend one and say why (the strongest single
image, the clearest verb). The user picks, and their own idea always goes first. (Model Kombat was the requester's
own idea; Meta Gear Solid was picked over Slop Invaders and a Frogger about waiting for GTA 6.)

## Parody rules, and why

- **No logos or brand marks.** Draw the shapes (a Wayfarer frame, a car's silhouette), never the badge. Names of
  companies may appear as facts ("CPU · OPENAI").
- **No made-up quotes in real mouths.** Real words only as reported, with the source; jokes belong to the game's voice.
- **Even-handed.** In a feud, the same health, speed and frame data; mirrored specials; as many punchlines each. Say so
  in the README.
- **True facts.** Dates, numbers and statements on screen match your sources; keep the sources in the README.
- **Public figures and public moments only.** Never a private person.
- **The disclaimer where it belongs**: "fan-made parody, not affiliated with …" on the page beside the game and on the
  share card, not over the play area.
